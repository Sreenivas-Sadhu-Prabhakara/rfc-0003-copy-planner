## Longer community post

How can an early network document help people today? Copy Planner turns an idea from
RFC 3, “Documentation conventions,” into a small tool for libraries, schools, volunteer organizers and community print workshops.
Compare direct distribution to every reader with a modern hub model: one master to each recipient group plus local copies. Produce a per-group packing and reproduction checklist with copy and page counts. Inputs stay in the browser; you can download the result
and continue using the loaded page offline.

Every reader gets one copy and one reader keeps each master. Total printing is unchanged; only the copies dispatched by the origin decrease. This model excludes retained archive copies, spoilage, envelopes, cost and transport. It sends nothing and verifies no delivery. The official RFC status is UNKNOWN / Legacy, not HISTORIC;
this is not a current Internet standard. RFC 3 is obsoleted by RFC 10. RFC 10 is in turn listed as obsoleted by RFC 16. RFC 10 revises the note conventions and recipient list; RFC 16 adds an MIT recipient. These are historical records, not current contact or submission instructions.

## Small teaching exercise

Try 12 library readers, 18 school readers and 6 club readers with a four-page note. Compare origin dispatch with total printing. Then try one reader per group: does the group model still reduce dispatch?

Expected observation: The community example sends 3 masters and prints 33 copies locally. Both approaches produce 36 copies, or 144 page units. One reader per group produces no dispatch reduction.
