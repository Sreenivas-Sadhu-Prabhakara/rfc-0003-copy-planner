RFC 0003 / Copy Planner / 1969

One master.
Many local readers.

Sending a copy to every person is only one way to share a document. Plan a master for each group and work out the local printing.

Original educational adaptation · UNKNOWN / Legacy

Move the copying closer to readers.

From the document

An early idea.

RFC 3 described sending a copy to each person on its distribution list, with reproduction handled locally if needed. This small logistical idea lets distant readers share access without the author mailing every copy.

Our planner adapts that idea to groups such as libraries and schools. The source lists named recipients, including more than one at the same institution; grouping readers into hubs is a modern choice, not a claim about the historical list.

Model & limitations. Every reader gets one copy and one reader keeps each master. Total printing is unchanged; only the copies dispatched by the origin decrease. This model excludes retained archive copies, spoilage, envelopes, cost and transport. It sends nothing and verifies no delivery.

Source evidence, status and archive limitations

RFC 3, DISTRIBUTION (a copy to each listed recipient, with local reproduction if wanted); FORM and OTHER NOTES (titles need not uniquely identify notes).

Official status: UNKNOWN · Legacy stream. UNKNOWN is not the formal HISTORIC classification. These educational adaptations are not current Internet standards.

RFC 3 is obsoleted by RFC 10. RFC 10 is in turn listed as obsoleted by RFC 16. RFC 10 revises the note conventions and recipient list; RFC 16 adds an MIT recipient. These are historical records, not current contact or submission instructions.

The retrieved errata view lists report 8412 as Reported, not Verified. Its cited section and text do not match the cached RFC, so it is not applied. Follow-up access to the newer endpoint was unavailable; check the linked page for the latest status.

The introductory affiliations and distribution list differ. We do not resolve those differences or reuse historical contact details.

Try it with someone

A two-minute experiment

Try 12 library readers, 18 school readers and 6 club readers with a four-page note. Compare origin dispatch with total printing. Then try one reader per group: does the group model still reduce dispatch?

Expected observation

The community example sends 3 masters and prints 33 copies locally. Both approaches produce 36 copies, or 144 page units. One reader per group produces no dispatch reduction.

Pass the idea along

A small tool for shared learning.
